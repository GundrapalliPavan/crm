PROJECT.md

Electrical Distribution CRM

Version: 1.1
Status: Product Definition
Project Type: Business CRM & Operations Platform
Initial Platform: Responsive Web Application
Future Platforms: Native Mobile App, Dealer Portal, Customer Portal, Vendor Portal
Primary Market: Electrical Product Distributors, Dealers & Wholesalers

⸻

1. Project Vision

Build a modern, premium, lightweight CRM and business operations platform for an electrical products distribution company.

The application should become the central operating system for the business.

It should bring together:

* Leads
* Customers
* Dealers
* Contacts
* Sales
* Quotations
* Orders
* Purchase Orders
* Billing
* Collections
* Inventory
* Team Activities
* Communication
* Customer Service
* Reporting

into one connected platform.

The objective is to reduce dependence on:

* Spreadsheets
* Personal notebooks
* WhatsApp groups
* Manual follow-up
* Disconnected billing systems
* Separate customer records
* Unstructured sales reporting
* Multiple disconnected communication tools

The platform should provide employees with one place to perform their daily work while giving management clear visibility into the business.

⸻

2. Product Positioning

This product should not feel like a traditional, complicated ERP.

It should combine the operational usefulness of a business management system with the simplicity and usability expected from modern SaaS products.

The experience should feel:

* Lightweight
* Premium
* Fast
* Modern
* Professional
* Reliable
* Easy to learn
* Easy to navigate
* Action-oriented

Users should require minimal training to perform their daily activities.

3. Platform Vision

The CRM should be designed as a multi-client business platform rather than only a desktop web application.

The initial product will be a responsive web application.

The platform should eventually support:

* Responsive Web Application — Initial
* Progressive Web App capabilities where practical
* Native Mobile Application — Future
* Dealer Portal — Future
* Customer Portal — Future
* Vendor Portal — Future
* External API/Integration Access — Future

All clients should operate on the same central business platform, business data, permissions, workflows, and backend services.

The web application is the first client of the platform, not the platform itself.

8. Core Product Principles

Business Value Over Feature Count

Do not introduce features simply because competing CRMs contain them.

Every feature should solve a real business problem.

⸻

Simplicity Over Complexity

Complex business processes should be presented through simple interfaces.

Hide unnecessary complexity.

Use progressive disclosure where appropriate.

⸻

Action Over Information

Whenever possible, information should lead directly to an action.

For example:

Outstanding Invoice

→ Send Reminder

Lead Requiring Follow-up

→ Call / WhatsApp / Schedule Follow-up

Low Stock

→ Create Purchase Request

⸻

Automation Over Repetition

If users repeatedly perform predictable actions, evaluate whether they can be automated.

⸻

Configuration Over Hardcoding

Business rules likely to change should be configurable where practical.

⸻

Mobile-Ready Thinking

Field-oriented workflows should be designed knowing that they may eventually be performed through a native mobile application.

⸻

Security by Default

Permissions, data isolation, auditability, and privacy should be considered during feature design rather than added later.

54. Initial Scope Discipline

The project has a broad long-term vision.

This does NOT mean every capability should be implemented in the first release.

Development should be phased.

Before implementing a capability, determine:

1. Is it required for the current business workflow?
2. Who needs it?
3. How frequently will it be used?
4. What problem does it solve?
5. Does another existing capability already solve the problem?
6. Is an integration preferable to building it internally?
7. Is it required now or merely useful in the future?

Avoid scope creep.

61. Working Instructions for Claude

When working on this project:

1. Read this PROJECT.md before making major product decisions.
2. Read PROJECT_SETUP.md before making technical or architectural decisions.
3. Understand the business problem before proposing a feature or interface.
4. Think from the perspective of the actual user performing the work.
5. Prefer simple workflows over feature-heavy interfaces.
6. Minimize clicks, unnecessary typing, and repetitive actions.
7. Identify automation opportunities when users perform repetitive work.
8. Consider WhatsApp, Email, SMS, Calls, In-App Notifications, and future Push Notifications where communication is relevant.
9. Consider third-party integrations before recommending that complex external functionality be rebuilt internally.
10. Keep business rules, security, permissions, and authoritative calculations in backend services rather than only in client applications.
11. Treat the web application as one client of the platform, not as the entire platform.
12. Keep future native mobile clients in mind when defining APIs and business workflows.
13. When designing field-sales workflows, consider whether they will eventually require a mobile-optimized experience.
14. Do not automatically replicate every web feature in the future mobile application.
15. Prioritize action-oriented mobile workflows for sales, visits, follow-ups, collections, communication, tasks, and field operations.
16. Do not introduce future mobile features into the current implementation scope unless explicitly requested.
17. Maintain architectural readiness without creating scope creep.
18. Do not prematurely implement AI features unless they solve a validated business problem.
19. Design for multi-branch and organizational scalability.
20. Consider permissions and data visibility for every important business workflow.
21. Avoid unnecessary technical or UX complexity.
22. Prefer reusable platform capabilities over duplicated module-specific implementations.
23. Explain important assumptions when requirements are unclear.
24. Do not silently expand the approved project scope.
25. Produce implementation-ready product thinking rather than only visually attractive screens.

62. Final Product Principle

The CRM should help users answer three questions:

What happened?

What needs my attention?

What should I do next?

Every major feature should contribute to answering at least one of these questions.

The ultimate objective is to create a CRM that employees rely on throughout their working day because it makes their work easier, while giving management accurate and timely visibility into the business.

Hide complexity. Preserve operational clarity.
